Avinya — change, and new beginnings

A collaboration between Shrujan and the LLDC that brings the artisans of Kutch together to make original work, rooted in their own perspective and their own hand.

  • 2–3Artisans per team
  • 12Craft traditions
  • 1969Building since
  1. 01 Heritage
  2. 02 Avinya
  3. 03 Gifting & Collaborations

Avinya puts artisans into teams of two and three and asks them to make something together — not to execute a design handed down to them.

For most of the women in these teams it is the first time they have worked this way: jointly, across craft traditions, with no brief to follow and no pattern to copy. The work that comes out of it is argued into being between them.

That is the point. Shrujan has worked since 1969 towards an empowered, self-reliant craft community, and a self-reliant maker is one whose own judgement is in the piece. Avinya is where heritage, contemporary design and the artisan’s own voice are allowed to meet without one being subordinated to the others.

How it works

Four stages, two or three hands

  1. 01

    Pairing

    Artisans are placed in teams of two or three, often alongside someone from a craft other than their own — an embroiderer with a weaver, a dyer with a printer.

  2. 02

    Co-creation

    The team develops the work together. Nothing is handed down to be executed; the idea is reached between them, and changes as they make it.

  3. 03

    Experiment

    Material, scale and technique are tested against each other. Some of the combinations that come out of it have not been attempted in Kutch before.

  4. 04

    Attribution

    The finished work carries the name of everyone who made it. The artisan’s voice stays attached to the piece after it leaves the studio.

For most of the women in these teams, it is the first time they have made something that was not asked for.
